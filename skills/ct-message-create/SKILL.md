# Core message (Thông điệp lõi)

A method by Cuong Thach. Full source: https://cuongthach.com/docs/content-system/idea-write-edit/guides/how-to/create-a-core-message/ (Vietnamese: https://cuongthach.com/docs/vi/content-system/idea-write-edit/guides/how-to/create-a-core-message/). When this skill and the docs disagree, the docs win.

Reply in the user's language. It belongs to the Idea step of **Idea - Write - Edit** (Ý - Viết - Sửa), in this order: a topic, every idea poured out, the core message, then the outline. So it comes **after the ideas are out**, usually right after a recording, and before the outline. If the ideas are not out yet, stop: this comes later.

## What it is

**One line** that is the nucleus of a post: which pain the customer is stuck on, what the writer's solution is, and what result it brings them. One post carries exactly one. No full stop in the middle, no "and" joining two halves, readable in one breath, because it sits on top of the outline and is read again and again while writing. From then until publishing, the title, the opening and the outline all follow it. Beyond the title, it never appears word for word in the post: the body spells out the pain, the solution and the result in full.

Everything written has one; only the scope changes. The whole website carries the brand's mission; every sales page leads with one (what problem the product solves, with what result); the product itself is a solution to a pain with a result; a post has its big message, and each bright idea repurposed on social media gets a small one. A docs page (Truth vault) needs none: it needs truth.

## Before you start

Ask the user for three things:

- **The ideas they poured out.** A recording transcript or notes on one topic, the more the better. What is not used is saved for a later post.
- **The customer entry of their brand profile.** The customer is the person who buys their product, already written there. Only one group is picked for this post.
- **Something they have done themselves.** The solution in the line must be theirs, so only they have it.

**Talk first, read later.** Do not read other people's posts or run Deep Research to find the message, and never suggest a solution: read first and that knowledge haunts the writing, so they write exactly what others wrote. Only when the user is stuck from the start do they learn more, until they feel sure enough to record.

## Four steps to one line

The first three steps happen on paper, with the user; only the last one leans on AI.

1. **Settle on one customer group.** Take the customer from the brand profile and pick one group for this post, named with a clear label, like "retired people who still want to learn" or "eager young people short of money". A product usually has a main customer and a secondary one: the post serves the main one, the secondary one gets its own post. Two groups in one post have different inner pains and no shared root: that is two posts.
2. **Take the pain apart into three layers.** The most important part and the one that takes the most work. Write three lines, in the user's words:
   - **Outer:** the symptom you can see, in the words the customer says, like "I can't write".
   - **Inner:** how they feel carrying that symptom: not good enough, behind colleagues, behind people online.
   - **Root:** the real cause the customer never sees on their own, which the writer sees as the one who went before: no direction, no formula, no template, no way to practice.

   A layer the user cannot see yet gets a `?`, never a guess.
3. **Write down the solution and the result.** The solution is the user's own way of solving it at the root, taken from real experience; if it is still rough, keep it rough. For a Know post that only explains a concept, the solution is the way they help readers understand fastest: examples, pictures, video, a short post, no lofty terms. The result is what the customer gets after using the solution, the one thing the user dares to promise; for a Know post, the result that can be measured is readers clicking through to the Trust post. After this step, all four parts of the context are there.
4. **Squeeze it into one line.** Join the last three parts: what the customer is stuck on, which solution the user uses, what result it brings. No full stop in the middle, no "and" joining two halves, readable in one breath. This is the step for AI: with all four parts as context, give a few versions by these rules and let the user pick one. A whole recording pasted with no context gives back lines that sound like ads. The line picked goes on top of the outline frame.

## Four checks

Read the line and ask four things. Each "no" has its own fix:

| Question | If it fails |
|---|---|
| One line, with no "and"? | Two halves joined by "and" or a full stop mean two topics: cut back to one half, or admit it is two posts |
| One customer group? | Keep the main customer; the other group gets another post |
| One message? | Pick one and split the other off |
| Has nobody said this exact line? | The user sounds like someone else: change the wording to their own. One matching sentence does no harm, unless it decides the whole idea |

When all four pass, the line is the core message, and the title and the opening come out of it.

## Once you have the line

- **Title:** the outer pain in the customer's words; often the title is the core message itself.
- **Opening:** promises the result from the root cause, says a little about the solution, and names the customer and their pain. After the opening, the inner pain is told through the writer's own story, with no promise there.
- **Outline:** pull the ideas the user poured out in around the line. An idea that serves it only halfway is thought through until it is whole: take ten of thirty ideas, find 11 and 12 missing, add exactly those two. Never force thirty ideas to fit.
- **Edit step:** the line is the yardstick for the title, the description, the opening and the main content; all four must say the same thing.

Know, Trust and Love posts build the line the same way and only put a different layer of the pain first: a Know post the inner pain, then the root (readers nod out of empathy; the post is short, with examples and pictures); a Trust post the outer pain (solve it right away, step 1, 2, 3); a Love post the root.

### Example

The post "The Idea - Write - Edit process: escape the never-ending writing loop" was built from this context:

| Part | Answer |
|---|---|
| Customer | The person who "keeps writing and never finishes" a post |
| Outer pain | One post edited three to ten times, three to ten times slower than others |
| Inner pain | Looking back, the post and the title no longer match: "Oh, where did my message go?" |
| Root pain | Doing the idea, the writing and the editing all at once |
| Solution | Three steps with stopping points: a firm script, a free-flowing draft with no judging, an edit that only trims spare words |
| Result | Ideas that sat in storage too long get out onto the website |
| **Title** | **Escape the never-ending writing loop**: the outer pain, in the customer's words |
| **Core message** | Three stages with stopping points get the stuck ideas of a writer who never finishes out onto the website |

The post's description still keeps a long sentence with an "and" joining two results; the message line keeps only the last result, because "escape the loop" already sits in the title.

## When it falls short

- **The line is long and needs an "and":** two topics, or the result is not clear so more keeps getting stuffed in. Keep one half and save the other.
- **No solution of their own:** common in Know posts. The solution of an explaining post lives in the examples, the pictures and the way the problem is rebuilt, not in a new formula.
- **Readers don't nod:** the wrong customer group, or the inner pain belongs to another group. Back to step 1.
- **The message loses its value midway:** the solution is outdated, or the problem is too big to solve. Put the post away; a different message is a different problem and a different solution, so a completely different post. Do not make one that looks like the old one.

Questions the user often asks: the core message comes **after** the recording (before it, a rough sense of what problem the topic solves and what the result is suffices), and the recording confirms it. A rough solution is still shared: there is no absolute right solution, only one that fits and works, and readers value a real journey over a perfect but hollow result.
